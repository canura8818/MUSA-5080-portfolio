Where is the model wrong?

Key Concepts

  • Problem: small neighborhoods sometimes not included in training set
  • Solution: merge small groups before CV

  • Filter out sale prices (self-determined so write down)
  • Buffer count vs kNN distance
    • Compare with CV RMSE
    • Are there error clusters?
      • If near houses have related errors, the model is missing something about place.

Tober’s First Law of Geography

  • All things are related but near things are more related
  • Spatial Lag: average of neighbor values (either Queen or Rook)
  • Slope of spatial lag vs residual plot gives Moran’s I
  • Moran’s I: “Am I above the mean and are my neighbors above the mean?”
  • High Moran’s I:
    • An amenity or disamenity you haven’t measured → spatial feature
    • Something about the whole neighborhood → neighborhood fixed effect
    • A relationship that differs by place → interaction

Right tool for inference about spillovers. For prediction it fails three ways: - It needs neighbors’ prices. To price a house, you’d need the prices of houses that haven’t sold yet. - It leaks. In CV, a test house’s neighbors in the training set carry its answer. - It explains nothing. “Near expensive houses” is not a reason a policymaker can act on. - Our rule: spatial features of X, never Y. Crime near the house is fine; prices near the house are not.

I Pattern For our errors
near +1 similar values cluster the model is missing something about place
near 0 no spatial pattern the errors look like noise in space
below 0 neighbors differ rare for errors; check your weights

Monte-Carlo Simulation

  • moran.mc
  • Randomly assign residuals to different samples –> calculate Moran’s I
  • Rank Moran’s I with different permutations (proxy p-value)
